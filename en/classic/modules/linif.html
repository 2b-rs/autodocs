Mediates LIN frames between upper layers and LIN driver and controls schedules, sleep, wake-up, and LIN TP.

The LIN Interface connects the upper communication modules with the LIN driver and transceiver and also contains the LIN transport protocol. As master, a channel processes schedule tables selected via LinIf_ScheduleRequest [SWS_LinIf_00202]. As slave, on the other hand, it only responds to header notifications from the driver [SWS_LinIf_91004]. In addition, it controls sleep and wake-up per channel, diagnostic transmission, and, in the slave, node configuration. Which services are available depends on the configured node type of the channels.

The LIN Interface (LinIf) mediates between the upper communication modules and the LIN driver and at the same time contains the LIN transport protocol [SWS_LinIf_00351]. Toward upper layers, in R20-11 it calls the configurable callouts <User_TriggerTransmit>, <User_TxConfirmation>, and <User_RxIndication> [SWS_LinIf_00528] [SWS_LinIf_00529] [SWS_LinIf_00530]; the user is the PduR or a CDD. It reports mode switches back to the LinSM via <User>_…Confirmation callouts [SWS_LinIf_00521]. Toward lower layers, it uses Lin and optionally LinTrcv [SWS_LinIf_00359].

After LinIf_Init [SWS_LinIf_00198], each channel is in LINIF_CHANNEL_SLEEP. LinIf only processes frames in LINIF_CHANNEL_OPERATIONAL SWS LinIf p. 45. A dedicated MainFunction per channel performs the work [SWS_LinIf_00384].

Master and slave operate fundamentally differently. A master channel processes schedule tables, selected via LinIf_ScheduleRequest with index 1..255; 0 represents the NULL_SCHEDULE [SWS_LinIf_00197] [SWS_LinIf_00202]. LinIf_Transmit only marks sporadic frames in this context; LinIf retrieves the data only when the slot is due [SWS_LinIf_00706]. A slave, in contrast, only responds to LinIf_HeaderIndication. Based on the PID, it decides whether to transmit, receive, or ignore [SWS_LinIf_00738] [SWS_LinIf_00733] [SWS_LinIf_00748], and manages the response_error signal autonomously [SWS_LinIf_00763].

A slave enters sleep after a go-to-sleep command or after a bus idle timeout [SWS_LinIf_00750] [SWS_LinIf_00755]. It confirms wake-up only upon the first received header [SWS_LinIf_00761]. For diagnostics, LinTp in the master switches the schedules via BswM_LinTp_RequestMode [SWS_LinIf_00629]. In the slave, LinIf serves node configuration according to ISO 17987-3 [SWS_LinIf_00771]. LinIf reports errors to the DET, such as LINIF_E_RESPONSE [SWS_LinIf_00729]. In case of timeouts, it aborts TP transmissions with E_NOT_OK [SWS_LinIf_00801] [SWS_LinIf_00806].

Role of the LIN Interface between users, driver, and transceiver

The LIN Interface (LinIf) mediates between the upper communication modules and the LIN driver. At the same time, it contains the LIN transport protocol: its services such as LinTp_Init and LinTp_Transmit are also available via LinIf.h [SWS_LinIf_00350] [SWS_LinIf_00351]. Toward lower layers, LinIf uses driver services such as Lin_GoToSleepInternal, Lin_Wakeup, and Lin_WakeupInternal [SWS_LinIf_00359]. Optionally, the LIN Transceiver Driver is added, whose mode and wake-up services LinIf maps to a unified interface for all transceivers SWS LinIf p. 24. For this purpose, LinIf_SetTrcvMode, LinIf_GetTrcvMode, LinIf_GetTrcvWakeupReason, and LinIf_SetTrcvWakeupMode forward the calls to the transceiver driver [SWS_LinIf_00544] [SWS_LinIf_00545] [SWS_LinIf_00547] [SWS_LinIf_00550].

Toward upper layers, LinIf calls its users via configurable callouts: <User_TriggerTransmit>, <User_TxConfirmation>, and <User_RxIndication> [SWS_LinIf_00528] [SWS_LinIf_00529] [SWS_LinIf_00530]. The user is either the PduR, in which case PduR_LinIfTriggerTransmit must be entered [SWS_LinIf_00722], or a CDD whose callback header LinIf includes [SWS_LinIf_00669]. LinIf reports mode switches back via <User>_ScheduleRequestConfirmation, <User>_GotoSleepConfirmation, and <User>_WakeupConfirmation [SWS_LinIf_00520] [SWS_LinIf_00521] [SWS_LinIf_00522]. The receiver of these notifications is the LinSM SWS LinSM p. 24. In addition, LinIf requires the services BswM_LinTp_RequestMode, Com_SendSignal, and Det_ReportError [SWS_LinIf_00360]. Starting with R24-11, the LSduR replaces the PduR or CDD as upper user SWS LinIf p. 1. For R20-11, the <User_…> callouts apply.

Each channel is either master or slave. Slave services such as LinIf_HeaderIndication or LinIf_GetConfiguredNAD exist only if at least one slave channel is configured [SWS_LinIf_00863] [SWS_LinIf_00861]. LinIf_ScheduleRequest exists only if at least one master channel is configured [SWS_LinIf_00858]. Cyclic processing runs in LinIf_MainFunction_<LinIfChannel.ShortName> [SWS_LinIf_00384]. This function exists once per channel SWS LinIf p. 119 and is called with the period LinIfMainFunctionPeriod. In the master, it queries the Schedule Table Manager; in the slave, it monitors timing conditions SWS LinIf p. 45. Bus mirroring is enabled per channel with LinIf_EnableBusMirroring; after that, LinIf reports received and transmitted frames to the Mirror module [SWS_LinIf_00876] [SWS_LinIf_00838] [SWS_LinIf_00873].

Initialization and state machines of channel and LinTp

LinIf_Init receives a pointer to LinIf_ConfigType [SWS_LinIf_00198] [SWS_LinIf_00668] and transitions the module from LINIF_UNINIT to LINIF_INIT SWS LinIf p. 47. Each channel has its own sub-state machine. After initialization, it is in LINIF_CHANNEL_SLEEP without a go-to-sleep command having been sent. LinIf processes headers and responses only in LINIF_CHANNEL_OPERATIONAL SWS LinIf p. 45. Master channels start with the NULL_SCHEDULE SWS LinIf p. 90. Slave channels take over the initial and configured NAD as well as the PIDs from the configuration [SWS_LinIf_00767] [SWS_LinIf_00768] and provide the LIN Product Identification [SWS_LinIf_00769].

State diagram: Lifecycle of LinIf and channel sub-states; initialization leads to the sleep state, wakeup and sleep transition between channel states. Sources: SWS LinIf p. 45 [SWS_LinIf_00204] [SWS_LinIf_00205] [SWS_LinIf_00755]

The transport protocol has its own state machine per channel. LinTp_Init [SWS_LinIf_00350] sets LINTP_INIT with the sub-state LINTP_CHANNEL_IDLE [SWS_LinIf_00710] SWS LinIf p. 99. After LINTP_CHANNEL_BUSY, the channel transitions as soon as an FF or SF is detected as a TP message [SWS_LinIf_00322] [SWS_LinIf_00323] or LinTp_Transmit is called SWS LinIf p. 108. LinTp_Shutdown [SWS_LinIf_00355] closes all open connections and sets all channels to LINTP_UNINIT SWS LinIf p. 102. Whether LinTp is included at all is determined by LinIfTpSupported at compile time SWS LinIf p. 61.

Master Channel: Schedule Tables Determine Bus Traffic

In the master, the schedule table forms the basis of all communication. Each channel has an empty NULL_SCHEDULE, and the intervals between frames are multiples of LinIfMainFunctionPeriod SWS LinIf p. 44. LinIf_SchHandleType encodes the NULL_SCHEDULE as 0x00 and the remaining tables as index 1..255. The index is unique per channel, but not per ECU [SWS_LinIf_00197]. LinIf_ScheduleRequest operates asynchronously [SWS_LinIf_00202]; <User>_ScheduleRequestConfirmation reports the completed switch [SWS_LinIf_00520]. In the configuration, each table is assigned a LinIfRunMode, namely RUN_CONTINUOUS or RUN_ONCE ECUC-Parameter p. 265. The parameter Schedule was only renamed in R22-11 SWS LinIf p. 1.

LinIf_Transmit [SWS_LinIf_00201] does not trigger immediate transmission. It only marks sporadic frames (master) or event-triggered frames (slave) as pending SWS LinIf p. 79. For other frames, it still returns E_OK [SWS_LinIf_00700], whereas during a NULL_SCHEDULE it returns E_NOT_OK [SWS_LinIf_00719]. If a transmission slot is due, LinIf retrieves the data via <User_TriggerTransmit> and passes it to the driver using Lin_SendFrame SWS LinIf p. 37. If the user returns E_NOT_OK, the frame is omitted [SWS_LinIf_00706]. LinIf checks the outcome using Lin_GetStatus, at the earliest after the maximum frame length and at the latest at the next table entry SWS LinIf p. 35. LIN_TX_ERROR and LIN_TX_BUSY lead to <User_TxConfirmation> with E_NOT_OK [SWS_LinIf_00728]; LIN_TX_OK leads to E_OK SWS LinIf p. 40.

Sequence diagram: Notification, slot-controlled data retrieval, and status evaluation during master transmission. Sources: [SWS_LinIf_00201] [SWS_LinIf_00706] [SWS_LinIf_00728] SWS LinIf p. 125 SWS PduR p. 107

Slave channel: Reaction to master headers

A slave has no schedule. It reacts exclusively to LinIf_HeaderIndication [SWS_LinIf_91004] SWS LinIf p. 39. LinIf first evaluates the PID:

  • Transmit frame: <User_TriggerTransmit> writes directly into the driver buffer [SWS_LinIf_00738]. Afterward, LinIf sets Cs and Dl and reports LIN_FRAMERESPONSE_TX [SWS_LinIf_00739]. If the user returns E_NOT_OK, LinIf reports LIN_FRAMERESPONSE_IGNORE [SWS_LinIf_00740].
  • Receive frame: LinIf reports LIN_FRAMERESPONSE_RX [SWS_LinIf_00733].
  • Irrelevant frame: LinIf reports LIN_FRAMERESPONSE_IGNORE [SWS_LinIf_00748].

After successful reception, the driver calls LinIf_RxIndication, and LinIf forwards the data to <User_RxIndication> [SWS_LinIf_91005] [SWS_LinIf_00734]. After successful transmission, the driver calls LinIf_TxConfirmation, and LinIf confirms with E_OK [SWS_LinIf_91006] [SWS_LinIf_00741]. For event-triggered frames, the PID of the associated unconditional frame is located in the first data byte [SWS_LinIf_00731]. The pending flag is cleared after successful transmission [SWS_LinIf_00732].

LinIf manages the response_error signal independently for each slave channel [SWS_LinIf_00763] and updates it via Com_SendSignal [SWS_LinIf_00764]. After successful transmission, it is cleared [SWS_LinIf_00747]. Any change can optionally be reported via <User_ResponseErrorSignalChanged> [SWS_LinIf_00765] [SWS_LinIf_00766]. NAD and the PID list can be read and written at runtime [SWS_LinIf_00829] [SWS_LinIf_00833] [SWS_LinIf_91002] [SWS_LinIf_91003]. The PIDs of MRF and SRF remain excluded [SWS_LinIf_00809] [SWS_LinIf_00824]. A typical source of these values is non-volatile memory SWS LinIf p. 57.

Sleep and wakeup of a LIN channel

The master transmits a go-to-sleep command upon request. The slave goes to sleep when it receives this command or when the bus remains inactive SWS LinIf p. 48. In the slave, an MRF with NAD 0 is considered a go-to-sleep command [SWS_LinIf_00750]. Bus idle monitoring runs in state LINIF_CHANNEL_OPERATIONAL [SWS_LinIf_00751] [SWS_LinIf_00752] [SWS_LinIf_00753]. Each driver callback restarts the timer [SWS_LinIf_00754]. In both cases, LinIf calls <User>_GotoSleepIndication [SWS_LinIf_00756]. If the user subsequently calls LinIf_GotoSleep [SWS_LinIf_00204], Lin_GoToSleepInternal follows immediately [SWS_LinIf_00757]. Afterward, LinIf clears the wakeup flag [SWS_LinIf_00758] and confirms with TRUE [SWS_LinIf_00759]. In the master, according to the diagram, the MainFunction transmits the command via Lin_GoToSleep and confirms after the status check SWS LinIf (Abb.) p. 93 [SWS_LinIf_00521].

Sequence diagram: Transition of a slave channel into the sleep state after a go-to-sleep command or bus idle timeout. Sources: [SWS_LinIf_00750] [SWS_LinIf_00755] [SWS_LinIf_00756] [SWS_LinIf_00757] [SWS_LinIf_00759] SWS LinIf p. 137

Wakeup originates either from the user via LinIf_Wakeup or from a detected bus wakeup. LinIf checks for a bus wakeup via LinIf_CheckWakeup SWS LinIf p. 51. For this purpose, LinIf_CheckWakeup [SWS_LinIf_00378] calls Lin_CheckWakeup or LinTrcv_CheckWakeup SWS LinIf p. 112. The drivers report a detected wakeup via LinIf_WakeupConfirmation [SWS_LinIf_00715], whereupon LinIf sets the channel's wakeup flag SWS LinIf p. 98. LinIf_Wakeup [SWS_LinIf_00205] behaves differently depending on the state. On an awake channel, it simulates success. On a sleeping channel without a wakeup flag, it calls Lin_Wakeup SWS LinIf p. 89. If the wakeup flag is set, Lin_WakeupInternal is likely to activate the channel without a wakeup pulse [SWS_LinIf_00359].

The confirmation in the master originates from the MainFunction SWS LinIf (Abb.) p. 87. In the slave, it comes with the first received header [SWS_LinIf_00761]. Before returning E_NOT_OK, LinIf reports FALSE [SWS_LinIf_00762]. If the master transmits no headers, confirmation is omitted and the LinSM times out SWS LinIf p. 52. If a wakeup request arrives during sleep transition, the master remains operational SWS LinIf p. 56. In this case, the slave restarts wakeup SWS LinIf p. 53.

Sequence diagram: Wakeup check via EcuM, wakeup request by the user, and node-dependent confirmation. Sources: [SWS_LinIf_00378] [SWS_LinIf_00715] [SWS_LinIf_00205] [SWS_LinIf_00761] [SWS_LinIf_00762] SWS EcuM p. 182

Diagnostics: LIN TP and Node Configuration

In the master, LinTp controls the schedule tables via BswM_LinTp_RequestMode with LinTp_Mode [SWS_LinIf_00629], provided that LinTpScheduleChangeDiag is active. Upon LinTp_Transmit [SWS_LinIf_00351], LinTp requests the diagnostic request schedule. The diagnostic response schedule follows after the physical request, and the applicative schedule follows after the response SWS LinIf p. 62. After a functional request, the channel returns to the previous schedule [SWS_LinIf_00707]. PduR_LinTpTxConfirmation follows only after the schedule switch has ended [SWS_LinIf_00702]. A new physical request terminates the ongoing TP processing [SWS_LinIf_00708]. LinTp_ChangeParameter [SWS_LinIf_00501] is only a placeholder and rejects any change SWS LinIf p. 103.

Sequence diagram: TP transmission in the master with schedule switching via BswM and LinSM. Sources: [SWS_LinIf_00351] [SWS_LinIf_00702] [SWS_LinIf_00878] SWS LinIf (Abb.) p. 90 SWS LinIf p. 139

LinIf must be ready for reception at all times. An FF or SF starts the reception via PduR_LinTpStartOfReception. In doing so, LinIf maps the NAD to an N-SDU ID and copies each frame using PduR_LinTpCopyRxData SWS LinIf p. 67. In the slave, the transport layer and Node Configuration share the MRF and SRF frames SWS LinIf p. 54. Therefore, LinIf evaluates the NAD, PCI, and SID of each MRF [SWS_LinIf_00771] and distributes it accordingly [SWS_LinIf_00772] [SWS_LinIf_00773] [SWS_LinIf_00774]. An SRF header is handled by the node configuration handler if a response is pending; otherwise it goes to the transport layer [SWS_LinIf_00775] [SWS_LinIf_00776]. The following services are supported:

  • \"Assign NAD\", optional [SWS_LinIf_00810] [SWS_LinIf_00779]
  • \"Assign Frame ID range\" [SWS_LinIf_00811] [SWS_LinIf_00781]
  • \"Save Configuration\" via <User_SaveConfigurationRequest> [SWS_LinIf_00812] [SWS_LinIf_00857]
  • \"Read by Identifier\" [SWS_LinIf_00813] [SWS_LinIf_00841]

Positive responses go out with the next SRF header [SWS_LinIf_00780].

Behavior in the event of an error

LinIf reports development errors to the DET, including with LINIF_E_UNINIT, LINIF_E_NONEXISTENT_CHANNEL, LINIF_E_PARAMETER, LINIF_E_PARAM_POINTER, and LINIF_E_SCHEDULE_REQUEST_ERROR [SWS_LinIf_00376]. The callbacks check channel and pointer [SWS_LinIf_00843] [SWS_LinIf_00844]. LinIf ignores unexpected calls of LinIf_RxIndication or LinIf_TxConfirmation without a preceding header [SWS_LinIf_00848] [SWS_LinIf_00852].

As runtime errors, LinIf recognizes LINIF_E_RESPONSE and LINIF_E_NC_NO_RESPONSE [SWS_LinIf_00729]. If the driver calls LinIf_LinErrorIndication in the slave, the frame is considered lost. LinIf then reports LINIF_E_RESPONSE, except for LIN_ERR_HEADER [SWS_LinIf_00735] [SWS_LinIf_00743]. In addition, it confirms the transmission attempt with E_NOT_OK [SWS_LinIf_00742] and sets the response_error signal in case of response errors [SWS_LinIf_00736] [SWS_LinIf_00744]. A new header while a response is still expected invalidates the old frame [SWS_LinIf_00846]. When mirroring is active, LinIf reports such errors with a mapped status code [SWS_LinIf_00869] [SWS_LinIf_00870].

LinTp monitors three time limits and aborts with E_NOT_OK to the PduR when they are exceeded:

  • N_As [SWS_LinIf_00799] [SWS_LinIf_00801]
  • N_Cs [SWS_LinIf_00802]
  • N_Cr [SWS_LinIf_00804] [SWS_LinIf_00806]

LinTp also aborts in case of buffer problems [SWS_LinIf_00676] [SWS_LinIf_00677]. In the master, this only occurs after returning to the applicative schedule [SWS_LinIf_00792] [SWS_LinIf_00879]. If the buffer is just barely insufficient, the master holds back the SRF headers [SWS_LinIf_00679] SWS LinIf p. 65. Alternatively, LinIf retries the copy attempt with length 0 until space is available or the next CF arrives [SWS_LinIf_00793] [SWS_LinIf_00795]. Furthermore, aborts with E_NOT_OK occur upon an incorrect NAD [SWS_LinIf_00655], an incorrect sequence number [SWS_LinIf_00081], an FF or SF after a CF [SWS_LinIf_00653], and an error on the SRF response [SWS_LinIf_00796]. Upon a P2 timeout, LinIf requests the applicative schedule [SWS_LinIf_00877]. If LinIf_ScheduleRequest rejects the switch, LinTp_Transmit returns E_NOT_OK [SWS_LinIf_00878].

In Node Configuration, LinIf aborts a pending response if N_As expires [SWS_LinIf_00785] [SWS_LinIf_00786], if an unknown NAD arrives [SWS_LinIf_00787], or if a new valid request arrives [SWS_LinIf_00789]. In contrast, a corrupted MRF leaves the pending response in place [SWS_LinIf_00791]. The extended production errors for LinTp timeouts and rejected schedule switches were only added with R23-11; they do not apply to R20-11 SWS LinIf p. 1 SWS LinIf p. 74.
